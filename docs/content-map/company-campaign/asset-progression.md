# Asset progression and cooperative content

[Company overview](README.md) · [Assets and economy](assets.md) · [Contracts](contracts.md)

Proposed gameplay exploration. Cross-run equipment, facilities, field services and realm apparatus are not implemented by this work. Native locations and selected existing effects supply the starting point.

Persistent assets require a guild. Material pickups belong to individual players; chosen contributions build facilities benefiting all members. [Guild resources](guild-resources.md) defines this economy. [Inheritance and separate returns](inheritance-extraction-recovery.md) defines how useful hauls survive mixed co-op outcomes.

Concrete next design: [eight building tracks and costs](buildings.md), [equipment/service recipes](equipment-recipes.md) and [worked return budgets](guild-economy.md). Early Armory/Smithy/Drill-yard choices lead to rune-stage support or logistics, then normal-endgame masterworks and optional extended protection/return apparatus. The catalogue is an initial proposal, not implemented progression or a required route through every building.

## Capability, reach and return

The company begins by preserving useful finds. It develops reliable loadouts and magic access, then field logistics, then equipment/services for ambitious realm expeditions. Progress appears on the next hero's equipment, starting skills, available spells and usable actions.

This is not a required sequence of mission completions. A capable group can make a deep profitable expedition early; another group can build a useful armory through shorter runs. An Orb win remains a distinct native outcome.

| Stage | Useful returns | Tangible company development | Cooperative content that makes it useful |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D:1-7 | Personal timber/metal/stone finds, desirable gear, consumables and early magic | First contributed Armory; actual inherited equipment, then funded Library/training | Carry a teammate's tagged share, cover a retreat, extract separately or keep pushing |
| Lair / Orc | Hide/fibre, metal and reagents alongside specialized gear, manuals/research and devices | Forge/Apothecary, stronger preparation and a supplied branch-side contact | Protect a resource cache or return approach; divide equipment and support responsibilities |
| First S-branches | Resistance gear, valuable equipment and branch-relevant knowledge | Reliable resistance/mobility preparation and planned supplies | Allocate scarce protection, separate support threats and hold an approach while another hero moves |
| Elf / Vaults / Depths | Crystal, larger common-material caches, advanced magic, artifacts and cooperative equipment | Specialized party builds, improved facilities and kits for different threats | Linked enemies, competing lanes, support range versus flanking, retrieval of a valued loadout |
| Third rune / Zot | High-value gear, protection and final-build tools | A party deliberately prepared for its selected route | Preserve escape resources, arrange a gear change and safeguard rare deployed equipment |
| Orb escape | Surviving eligible gear/haul under a future terminal adapter | Preserve major equipment and finance further development | Native multiplayer ending must be characterized; do not assume every hero independently escapes |
| Hells | Realm-specific apparatus/knowledge and rare gear | Equipment/devices that materially counter selected constraints | Assign ward-bearing, recovery, interruption and escort responsibilities |
| Pan / Tomb | Planar/ritual technology, rare equipment and valuable research | Controlled return, stronger salvage and advanced expedition tools | Commit a return site, protect activation and recover assets before using the final escape |
| Ziggurats / pinnacle | Exceptional risky hauls and rare equipment | Mature reserves, experimental builds and potentially advanced deployment | Allocate a finite expedition supply; send valuables home or risk valuable kits deeper |

## Early play: equipment creates a company immediately

The guild need not wait for a first rune to grow. The first secured materials can finance a shared Armory; a ring or weapon can wait safely packed until that service is built. A one-member guild can use the same loop. New members benefit from an established guild's buildings immediately.

**Episode:** a pair finds a good polearm and an escape consumable. One hero can use the weapon now; a future character could use it on the next run. The pair can deposit it, keep using it or transfer it to a compatible teammate. A deposit gives up current use and secures future use. A native trade preserves field use but does not secure company custody.

Native inventory, hands, anatomy, armour burden and training remain relevant. Better funded initial training can expand which inherited loadouts are practical. A separate carrying-weight bar or arbitrary cargo-slot cap should be added only if actual inventory/asset decisions need it.

New members of an established company can borrow real protection and tools immediately. They can contribute by carrying a useful tool, covering an approach, controlling enemies or helping preserve the haul. They do not need to earn an achievement before receiving equipment the company wants them to use.

Encounter geometry, retreat and sparse service access can make bringing goods out interesting. Scatter finite materials in ordinary layouts and themed cache sites, without making every object a harvesting action. Reward desirable actual items and construction inputs; no extra mission checklist is required. Players decide whether to craft for themselves or contribute to the next shared facility.

## Lair and Orc: turn returns into infrastructure

A recovered manual can support a Training Hall curriculum. Contributed metal, stone and other materials can build a Forge or field service; keeping those personal materials can instead fund a compatible personal craft. The choice is voluntary and spends the same finite inputs once. Gold needs its separately specified shared-party allocation policy.

A useful outpost has a deposit service and specific stocked supplies. The company can send resistance and escape items to a declared compatible contact. The party decides whether to reach it together, secure an approach or keep its original plan and spend the money elsewhere.

The service can be placed in compatible branch generation for that company; it does not promise identical persistent coordinates across new seeds. A contested service can create an occasional readable encounter. Recapturing every installed service on every run would turn durable progress into maintenance work.

Secure deposit lets a hero send home a find and continue. Hero extraction settles that participant while peers continue. A survivor can bring out a finally fallen teammate's tagged materials and guild equipment; settlement follows the original owners. Closing a browser preserves native custody and control reservations rather than securing or surrendering loot.

## Construction connects the whole content curve

These are proposed sites and benefits, not existing authored encounters or tuned yields.

| Stage / site opportunity | Material choice | Shared capability it can finance | Co-op decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early stores / abandoned works | Common building bundles versus personal crafting inputs | First Armory, then a useful preparation building | One player returns with construction inputs while another continues; neither owns the other's share |
| Lair camps / Orc workshops | Fibre, metal and reagent stores | Forge, Apothecary and stocked Waystation | Secure an approach, collect different inputs, and decide which returned materials each player contributes |
| S-branch cache or recovery site | Common resources plus branch-relevant gear | Resistance/support production and better return access | Recover a member's actual bag or carry a useful consignment; avoid making every rune a building prerequisite |
| Elf / Vaults / Depths research site | Crystal, core materials and legitimate field research | Stronger Library/Training Hall and cooperative equipment works | Allocate finite devices, maintain formation or separate to retrieve another owner's property |
| Hells / Pan / Tomb installation | Specific apparatus component plus still-useful common materials | A defined realm protection, planar return or recovery service | Protect activation and decide who/cargo leaves first; no automatic remote-floor progress |
| Pinnacle attempts | Exceptional loot plus material reserves | Mature kits and optional advanced deployment | Risk the best shared kit or secure part of the haul for later members |

Higher-stage content supplies common inputs too; a mature guild need not return to the first floors to restock basic timber. Buildings expand the available preparations and actions. Repeatedly recapturing installed services or demanding a personal contribution score would weaken their shared benefit.

## Rune branches: prepare different parties

Generated water and poison branches already create different demands. A shared armory turns them into allocation decisions:

- Give a scarce resistance item to the frontliner or cover a vulnerable caster.
- Build around archived control/movement magic rather than waiting for another book drop.
- Commit real stock to a predictable contact instead of carrying every tool from D:1.
- Use a stronger inherited loadout to try a different branch order or approach.

Cooperative content can reward cross-lane support, separated threat priorities, an exposed route and a protected retreat. Its benefit can be an actual item, a useful component, safe access to stock or recovered property. Legitimate loot still matters when the party did not select a prescribed operation.

Use Swamp or Shoals and Snake or Spider as generated. Existing Slime commitments, rune/branch rules and already-generated endings still require honest native handling. Forest remains an additional content candidate after its separate generation/accounting frontier is resolved.

## Mature company equipment changes formations

Source contains the following cooperative leads. Persistence would let a company assemble strategies around them deliberately, instead of relying on those items appearing during this particular run.

| Source-backed lead | What a company holding can enable | Decision to explore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aegis of Fellowship | Issue a protective shield to the hero holding formation | Stay near protection or leave its scope to flank/scout/interrupt |
| Conduit Staff | Prepare a caster/support composition around ally-facing effects | Maintain useful proximity and MP support while handling spread threats |
| Aegis standard / astral beacon | Pack reusable support evokers with actual charge/recharge state | Spend capacity on this fight or preserve it; assign who carries and operates the item |
| Cooperative potions/scrolls | Provision actual rally, swap, recovery or shared-vigour tools | Choose the bearer and a native-valid use; cover a vulnerable action |
| Twin-Soul / Dawnforged–Duskweaver resonance | Assemble compatible relic-bearing heroes | Proximity versus separation; acquiring/producing both legal items is a separate problem |

These are source-backed descriptions/effects, not fresh acquisition or balance verification. Paired-effect code does not prove natural generation of both items. Native floor/range, god, friendly-fire, source-identity and recharge rules need testing.

A library and evoker reserve can support fragile caster groups. Physical protection and recovery supplies can support melee-heavy groups. A formation item can give a newcomer a concrete protective contribution. The encounters should support several combinations without imposing a specific healing class.

## Third rune and Zot: risk the best kit deliberately

The party can deploy its strongest company equipment or preserve some of it. Equipment issued into one expedition is unavailable to another; finite supplies can run out.

Depositing a rare support item protects the next party but removes its current support. Retaining it increases current capability while leaving it exposed. This is an asset decision rather than an additional mission objective.

Zot and the Orb escape need a clear company settlement policy aligned with actual native terminal behavior. A partial expedition can be profitable, but asset return/withdrawal must never be recorded as an Orb win. Characterizing the existing multiplayer ending is a prerequisite for this adapter.

## Extended rewards should create new capabilities

These are proposed apparatus/services, not existing effects guaranteed by a material's name.

| Region | Return / investment to explore | Mechanical capability | Cooperative use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dis | Defensive mechanism plus Workshop investment | Manufacture corrosion-management equipment or a limited protection device | Assign scarce protection to a vulnerable role or preserve it for another expedition |
| Gehenna | Heat-resistant apparatus and production knowledge | Protect a limited scroll use from specified realm interference | Decide which critical scroll can be relied upon and who operates the apparatus |
| Cocytus | Thermal preparation apparatus | Permit a limited protected potion use under a specified cold restriction | Allocate rare recovery capacity; screen the user or carry complementary supplies |
| Tartarus | Anchoring/ritual knowledge and suitable equipment | A limited will/recovery ward or interruption device for a defined hostile effect | Choose a warded bearer or protect a critical action window |
| Pan | Planar mechanism and funded installation | Establish a scoped return apparatus with finite capacity and activation rules | Commit the site, protect activation, choose what to send home and whether to continue |
| Tomb | Ritual components and recovery research | Improve recovery of real lost gear or provide a finite native-compatible emergency service | Decide whether to expose a rescue team and its remaining equipment |
| Ziggurat / trials | Rare equipment and secured capital | Mature supplies, stronger preparation and potentially a finite between-stage consignment | Prepare a deep attempt, send valuables home or leave while the company is ahead |

The company is allowed to develop an advantage against a realm restriction. That can be a strong reward. Each effect must specify exactly what check it changes, who receives it, what it spends and when it expires. It need not disable an entire realm to matter.

A reliable planar return can likewise be powerful. It is a new travel/activation primitive with real lifecycle requirements. Independent floor clocks do not imply that an unattended apparatus advances on wall time or that other floors simulate its threat.

## Advanced progress reaches the next early game

An established company can issue real protection, funded preparation, archived spells and a support kit to a fresh party. The group can begin with combinations that were previously impossible or unreliable:

- Stored armour plus starting training makes a new frontliner practical earlier.
- Archived control magic supports a planned caster opening and later transition.
- An early support evoker or formation item changes pair tactics from the first floors.
- A known recipe replaces an ordinary lost component without finding the same exact drop again.
- A supplied field network supports a more ambitious route, then eventually an explicit advanced start.

Do not automatically raise every early enemy's stats to cancel these gains. Offer ambitious targets and deeper deployment for experienced groups while retaining an appropriate ordinary opening for a new recruit. Early runs can still bring back a particular useful asset; repetitive low-risk early farming should not be a compulsory entrance fee for every extended attempt.

## Worked company history

Illustrative proposed equipment and decisions; no observed run or balanced economy fixture is claimed.

| Expedition / investment | What changes now | What changes next time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early pair returns personal materials and a branded spear | Each owner chooses what to pledge; one funded Armory benefits all members | Actual inherited equipment becomes available; unused materials remain personal |
| Company funds a focused training curriculum | It delays some supply purchases | Fresh heroes using that preparation begin with stronger relevant skills |
| Returning player and newcomer borrow complementary kits | Newcomer gets useful protection immediately; veteran plans around reliable control | Further actual returns diversify the armory |
| Lair/Orc group establishes and supplies a contact | Deposit a rare find while continuing; retrieve a planned consignment | Future expeditions have a useful service with finite actual stock |
| Mature party deploys a cooperative shield/staff kit | Formation and separation matter in combat | Successful return preserves a reusable group strategy and its same equipment |
| One player returns, another finally falls, a survivor carries the bag | Early return stays safe; survivor can rescue separately owned contents | Guild loans return to issuer; personal materials return to owner; a later full wipe cannot undo secured property |
| A whole expedition closes with stranded eligible gear | Finance bounded recovery, use reserves or accept the selected loss policy | Successful salvage restores the same assets; permanent facilities/training/library remain intact |
| Hell/Pan expedition returns apparatus | Fund a defined protection or return capability | Another deep expedition gains a dependable tool it previously lacked |
| Company plans another fresh or advanced attempt | Allocate equipment, training and supply exposure | Progress changes the available builds, routes, actions and risks |

There is no requirement to select a quest before useful returns count. Goals emerge from actual company needs: a second resistance kit, a lost shield, more reliable magic access or capital for a planar installation.

## Exploration shortlist

1. **Materials, guild construction and returned gear:** personal collection, voluntary building contributions and clear co-op allocation; prove custody and separate hero returns.
2. **Library and starting training:** reliable build changes that survive gear loss; prove research provenance and honest native preparation.
3. **One supplied contact and cooperative equipment:** put preparation into field decisions and formations.
4. **Loss/recovery choice:** compare full loss, recoverable gear and insurance with the same valued loadout.
5. **One realm apparatus and advanced deployment:** ambitious mechanics; test each specific restriction, travel and start-state contract before multiplying variants.
