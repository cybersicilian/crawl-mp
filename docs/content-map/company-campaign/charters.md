# Charters from early Dungeon through extended

[Campaign design](README.md) · [Contracts and probes](contracts.md) · [Native progression atlas](../progression.md)

All named charters, rewards and encounter changes below are proposed. Native locations and system constraints come from the existing content map and the source boundary in [contracts](contracts.md). This is a content design, not a list of implemented missions.

## Five encounter families that can grow with the company

| Family | Decision it introduces | Solo solution | Cooperative opportunity | Later variation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signal | Fight through an advantage, interrupt its source or approach differently | Sequence an approach and interruption; accept the harder fight if appropriate | Distract, flank, cover and interrupt from different positions | Enemy support relationships in Vaults, Dis and Tomb |
| Routework | Spend current exposure/resources to change a later approach | Secure the route before committing to the next fight | Split a same-floor approach while preserving a rendezvous | Rune-ending approaches and planar wing commitments |
| Concord | Separate or disrupt linked threats, or commit to a changed survivor phase | Use movement/control, then choose a target | Different heroes occupy different angles and handle different priorities | Paired wardens, infernal defenses and ritual support |
| Recovery | Protect or consume a finite recovery opportunity | Stabilize/retrieve a scenario resource; use native personal recovery | Recover a teammate or protect the position needed to use a valid recovery tool | Realm-specific recovery constraints and extraction |
| Extraction | Leave with a completed objective while managing separation and resources | Plan an exit rather than clear every threat | Screen, scout, regroup and recover | Third-rune return, Orb escape and planar return |

These families should share objective state, authored configuration, eligibility and completion mechanisms. Their native enemy/terrain details vary by branch. A universal quest NPC, a new class system or a different interpreter for every charter is unnecessary.

## Stage-by-stage charter matrix

The “bank” column is the exact kind of fact the proposed completion system must establish. Recognition is limited to that fact. The company effect applies to a later launch or a named campaign transition.

| H14 — Tomb | **Last Choir:** interrupt a ritual relationship or accept the changed fight to preserve resources | Multiple threats can be handled from different angles; solo can sequence physical interruption; Silence spell is optional | Operation and declared escape/return → Choir discovery and possible extended campaign conclusion |

The branch adapter chooses the generated water and poison branches. A campaign never requires Swamp in a seed containing Shoals, or Snake in a seed containing Spider. Forest is a future optional third-rune adapter after the generation and rune-accounting frontier is resolved; H06 does not assume it is currently qualified.

H09-H14 are operation concepts inside or adjacent to native realm content. Required departure/return conditions must be stated in the charter and proved for that realm. Completing a script does not override Slime locks, Pan travel, Tomb topology or Hell recovery rules.

## H01 in detail: First Beacon

**State:** an optional D:3-7 vault has a route-chart beyond an enemy lookout and two approaches. A finite pack is already part of its encounter budget. The player sees what the lookout can alert before crossing the exposed approach.

**Choice:** accept the alerted pack, spend a disabling/movement resource on an interruption, or take the covered approach at the cost of position and actions. A durable build and a fragile build can make different sensible decisions.

**Co-op episode:** one hero draws the lookout's attention while a second approaches through cover. They choose whether to remain within support range or separate briefly. Sounding the alarm changes the pack's arrival/position once; it does not keep spawning XP.

**Solo episode:** the same hero can take the covered approach and interrupt before engaging, or accept a direct fight. No paired pressure plate, simultaneous button press or particular spell is needed.

**Consequence:** securing the chart and committing native progress durably banks H01. The Hall offers a Survey operation next; the hero can keep exploring immediately. There is one completion per operation identity, so a reload or another teammate interacting with the site does not award another discovery.

**Failure/exit:** ordinary bypass is valid. Irreversibly losing the objective after an explicit commitment can leave a bounded campaign complication. Merely passing the entrance or disconnecting does not count as failure. Objective destruction is a proposed mechanic and should be used only if it creates a clear decision; the first version can simply distinguish unattempted, completed and terminally incomplete.

**Reuses:** spatial generation, lookout/pack AI, noise, perception and normal native actions. A finite scripted alert, objective fact and durable banking are new contracts. Confirm the actual line-of-fire and AI behavior rather than trusting a transparent tile's appearance.

## H03/H04 in detail: first runes should change how the company works

The first-rune chapter is where an early company's practice meets a whole branch's resource pressure. Its operation should teach a reusable principle without forcing every rune attempt through a large side quest.

- **Waterline:** in Shoals, an exposed local objective opens a different approach to one ending section. In Swamp, a corresponding causeway/visibility problem serves the same role using that branch's native terrain/threats. The operation consumes real actions, safety and perhaps consumables. The direct rune path remains possible.
- **Coil and Web:** Snake uses a protected support threat; Spider uses a separated support/pressure relationship. A group decides whether to divide attention or concentrate on one target. A solo hero can manipulate the relationship in sequence.

Only the generated variant appears. A supported ending is selected before generation, or the campaign does not offer that promise. Changing an already generated ending in response to a late completion is outside the first design.

The first rune grants a native progress fact and a company reach distinction. Completing its optional operation additionally supplies a specific discovery. A group can therefore choose a clean rune attempt, an operation-focused attempt, or a later return under another charter. Do not require every rune to carry an additional compulsory checklist.

## H05 in detail: put the Waystation choice under pressure

The confrontation uses the linked-threat family from C5. The **survey commitment** gives the authored encounter an exposed optional flank that can change the link's geometry. The **recovery commitment** offers a local, finite opportunity that must be defended before it can support recovery.

These are mutually exclusive encounter configurations for the next chapter. Neither should be a passive stat multiplier or an unearned free revive. The ordinary native toolkit remains available in both.

A pair with good mobility can scout/flank while remaining vulnerable to isolation. A less experienced group may keep together to protect the recovery position. A solo hero has the same strategic choice with sequential execution. Success unlocks a future Concord operation, while the resolved campaign asset chooses which finale variation is offered.

If nobody becomes downed, a Recovery operation must still present a useful choice through securing, conserving or spending a scenario opportunity. No charter should require engineering an ally's death to demonstrate cooperation. Exactly what that opportunity can do must follow a tested native action; the campaign cannot invent an unlimited resurrection supply.

## H07: a complete normal-game ending

The ordinary campaign finale is an Orb escape. A company that finishes it has completed a meaningful campaign even if it never enters an extended realm.

The first version should recognize the actual native expedition outcome and optional verified cooperation facts. It must not assume that one hero physically owns a transferable Orb or that all participants can independently finish after the first escape. Current native/shared-state and terminal behavior need a dedicated characterization before any new extraction rule is selected.

Once that boundary is established, an extraction variant can ask whether to stay together, advance a scout, cover a junction or regroup after separation. It should not introduce a compulsory all-player attendance checkpoint or a timer on a floor that does not advance.

The normal finale offers **Conclude campaign** and **Begin an extended act later**. Native Orb escape still ends that hero's run. The extended act uses fresh expeditions; it does not resurrect the winning save or represent post-victory dungeon travel.

## Extended as a change in decisions

| Realm | Existing constraint to keep relevant | Proposed cooperative decision | Cross-run consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dis | Equipment corrosion and strong physical pressure | Separate linked defenders or commit to the protected fight; decide when to leave mutual support range | Unlock a linked-defense operation and an early introductory counterpart |
| Gehenna | Fire pressure and restricted scroll use | Spend exposure on an alternate approach or trust remaining native tools on the direct route | Choose a later interruption or direct-pressure chapter configuration |
| Cocytus | Cold pressure and restricted potion use | Hold a defensible recovery opportunity or preserve formation and momentum | Open a Recovery variant, not a global potion exception |
| Tartarus | Reduced will and undead/negative-energy threats | Interrupt support, isolate a threat or fight under its effect | Open a control/priority operation whose basic form can appear early |
| Pan | Realm itinerary, mutations, lord encounters and exit uncertainty | Choose one seal together or commit to separated same-floor tasks with a return plan | Open an authored route relationship for a future chapter, not guaranteed safe native travel |
| Tomb | Concentrated undead pressure and death/ritual threats | Divide attention among support sources or preserve resources for a direct approach | Resolve the campaign's ritual thread and open a simpler early counterpart |
| Pinnacle trial | Objective-specific pressure and leave/continue decisions | Choose a task that rewards the intended composition rather than a single maximum-damage build | A method-specific distinction and a finished campaign epilogue |

Realm progress broadens the company's charter library. A group can specialize in one Hell, choose contrasting realms or pursue a traditional fifteen-rune expedition. An extended campaign can conclude after its declared Hell/Pan/Tomb chapters without requiring every Hell or a Ziggurat.

Crucially, **a company collecting different runes across several expeditions has not achieved a fifteen-rune win**. Keep company discoveries, campaign chapters, and native one-expedition rune/escape distinctions separate in both presentation and predicates.

## The same content with one, two or four heroes

| Situation | One hero | Two heroes | Three or four heroes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signal | Select an approach and interrupt sequentially | Distract/flank or stay together | Cover more angles, with shared resource/space constraints |
| Routework | Persistent sequential latch; secure one approach before another | Parallel approaches with a rendezvous choice | A third optional threat/approach can add assignment variety if encounter budget supports it |
| Concord | Manipulate distance and target order | Separate/control threats from two angles | Split into smaller formations or protect a vulnerable interruption |
| Recovery | Stabilize or retrieve a scenario resource; native self-recovery | Cover a valid recovery action | Choose who leaves formation to help without making a permanent healer role |
| Extraction | Preserve a route and escape tools | Scout/screen versus mutual support | Decide between advance coverage and a safer convoy |

These are authored variations, not a claim that difficulty scales evenly with hero count. Record native bodies and authenticated controllers separately: a solo player with companions can use several bodies, but that is not evidence of multiple human controllers. Changing party size, being downed or disconnecting cannot redraw the reward budget or select a new easier variant mid-encounter.

## Worked campaign: The Lantern Compact

This sequence demonstrates four successful chapters plus one failed attempt. It is a proposed paper episode, not observed gameplay. An ordinary company can conclude at the normal finale; an expert company can select a deeper campaign starting point.

| Expedition | What happens inside the run | What persists | What the next group chooses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Founding | Two fresh heroes complete First Beacon at D:5. Completion banks while they are alive. One continues later to Lair; the company does not require the expedition to end. | Survey Table, route-chart provenance, Lantern distinction; chapter success | Undertake a first-rune preparation chapter using the surveyed route |
| 2 — First-rune attempt | A new pair commits to Waterline but dies before its goal is completed. H01's earlier discovery remains. | One Contested Approach complication for this chapter; no compensation currency or success proof | Retry the declared altered approach, or choose its bounded recovery variant |
| 3 — Recovery and preparation | A returning player and a newcomer launch fresh heroes. They use basic actions on the same operation family, resolve the recovery variant and obtain the declared first rune. | Runebearer reach, restored Waystation asset, contributor histories; complication cleared | Commit the Waystation to a surveying or recovery configuration for Twin Wardens |
| 4 — Confrontation | A three-hero group tackles the chosen linked-threat operation in Vaults/Depths. Objective success banks; the hero run can continue. | Warden's Standard, asset commitment/result and the campaign's finale configuration | Attempt Carry the Light with a composition suited to the available finale |
| 5 — Normal finale | A fresh expedition reaches three runes and completes the declared valid native Orb escape. | Orbwarden distinction, a completed normal campaign and its named lineage | Conclude; explore another commitment path in a new campaign; or begin the optional extended act |
| Optional 6 — Infernal | Fresh heroes complete one selected Hell operation and its declared return. | A specific realm discovery and Worldwalker evidence, not an inherited rune in the next run | Pursue a contrasting Hell or choose the Pan chapter |
| Optional 7 — Planar | A new expedition completes Concord of Gates under the fixed manifest. | Planar relationship and second extended chapter result | Choose the Tomb conclusion or the campaign's authored alternative |
| Optional 8 — Choir | Fresh heroes complete the declared Tomb objective and return/ending condition. | Deep Compact evidence if the Hell/Pan/Tomb predicates all hold; extended campaign conclusion | Archive, choose a new campaign path, or attempt a separate pinnacle trial |

No native hero inherits the previous hero's XP, piety, rune inventory or arbitrary equipment. The company inherits the changed choices and provenance. A current hero can complete an incidental deeper personal milestone while its campaign charter concerns an earlier chapter; those facts are still recognized, without pretending that a future ungenerated story operation was completed.

## Mixed experience and asynchronous contribution

An experienced company should always be able to select a lower-depth or short charter with a newcomer. That expedition can discover a new method, resolve a declared recovery chapter or add a distinct cooperative history. It should not be required to farm early charters to finance extended play.

Company-wide asynchronous goals can ask for distinct discoveries, such as a water-route proof and a linked-threat proof from separate expeditions. Those are expressly **company collection goals**. A joint maneuver requires its specified events in the same expedition, and a five-rune or fifteen-rune escape requires its specified native facts in one run.

One member can make progress solo while others are offline. Another can later use the discovered operation with a group. The offline member does not receive fictitious personal encounter credit; the company gains the discovery and every participant retains the events they actually earned.

## Content development order

Start with First Beacon, one generated-branch first-rune adapter, and Twin Wardens using the Waystation choice. These exercise early → middle progression and cross-run consequences. Add Carry the Light only after the existing multiplayer end condition is characterized. Add one Hell operation and one same-floor Pan operation before multiplying realm variants. Tomb and trial epilogues can then reuse the objective grammar, completion evidence and campaign transition mechanisms.

Every new charter should answer: **what plan changes for this build, what changes for a cooperating group, and which later choice is different because it succeeded?** If only the badge text changes, it does not fill the intended design space.
