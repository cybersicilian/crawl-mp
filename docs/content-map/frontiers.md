The [current campaign design](company-campaign/README.md) and [asset probe set](company-campaign/contracts.md#discriminating-assessments) set the immediate priority: native custody/return, fresh-party gear, library/training, field supplies and loss/recovery. ## Design gates for any new family

- The encounter/operation changes at least one real decision: target, position, route, resource allocation, timing, commitment or next-run choice.
- There are at least two explainable plans for appropriate builds; the variant is not a universal extra reward for doing the obvious thing.
- Ordinary progression stays viable solo. A party gains alternatives without needing a specific class, species, spell or god.
- The normal run has bounded length. Prefer replacing equivalent encounters or providing alternatives over stacking compulsory preparation.
- New conditions, costs and effects are visible at the decision point. Reversible view/filter actions apply immediately; consequential commitments retain an explanation and confirmation.
- State belongs to the right scope: hero, account, party, floor, encounter, expedition or campaign. Source, actor, recipient and owner are distinct fields where necessary.
- Native effects and objective states persist across saves and floors; outer completion does not duplicate through re-ingestion, reconnection or retries.
- Success and failure are observable. Missing telemetry is unknown; historical associations are not a live causal balance model.

## Probe A — Is the fork's extra route a complete route?

**Frontier:** branch/rune authoring → generated and correctly counted Forest route → more third-rune and intermediate progression options.

- **Competing explanations:** A1 Forest is a complete reachable rune branch; A2 only definitions/catalogue/end-map pieces are present; A3 it is reachable but inherited fifteen-rune predicates produce incorrect results.
- **Setup/control:** isolated disposable native game with fixed seed/content build; known-working ordinary Lair rune as control. No existing human save. First establish the build loads and the generator accepts the content.
- **Actions:** generate Vaults entrances over selected deterministic seeds; visit Forest, traverse four floors, reach its ending, collect the mossy rune; save/resume; revisit; inspect total and “all runes” state. Repeat one ordinary solo and one party run. Trace `MAX_RUNES`, `obtainable_runes`, score/output and any species/background completion predicates.
- **Eligible/ineligible cases:** Forest enabled and its entrance depth vs an unrelated branch/depth; ordinary three-rune access vs fewer than three; collected mossy rune vs merely seen rune.
- **Acceptance:** an actual entrance/ending/rune exists; identity and rune state survive saves; total possible runes is coherent; completion messages/score conditions reflect the selected rules. No claim of balance follows from generation.
- **Fail/inconclusive:** a parser or build failure is setup failure; a missing entrance under one seed is not proof of impossibility; a wrong total rules out coherent all-rune semantics for that build, not Forest's tactical promise.
- **Status here:** branch/catalogue/rune definitions and the fifteen-rune default were inspected; native route generation and collection were not run.

## Probe B — Do objectives change decisions for solo and co-op?

**Frontier:** existing combat/geometry → useful objective relationships → a reusable early/middle/late encounter family.

- **Competing explanations:** B1 the objective creates different viable plans; B2 all builds simply focus-fire or lure as before; B3 only a specific two-player composition can solve it.
- **Smallest setup/control:** paper map plus native action assumptions; compare an ordinary vault and a C2/C5 variant with the same approximate encounter/reward budget. Use an early melee hero, a fragile ranged hero and a two-hero pair as contexts. The baseline must itself be intelligible.
- **Actions/measurements:** ask for the first several actions, intended target, resource commitment, retreat condition and solo/party variation. Record the reason for the plan, not just a preference score. If plans differ on paper, select one isolated native encounter to test.
- **Acceptance:** at least two coherent plans respond to different state/build constraints; solo has a valid path; cooperation changes assignments/position or timing rather than only time-to-kill.
- **Native follow-up:** observe grates/line-of-fire, ally effects, threat telegraphs, source identity, repeat activation, delayed effects, enemy death, floor unload and reload. Use an ordinary spell/trade action as a known-working control and the nearest blocked-line/wrong-floor recipient as ineligible cases.
- **Fail/inconclusive:** UI inability to see the objective is a delivery failure; an already-obvious dominant solution contradicts the selected objective design, not all linked encounters.
- **Status here:** scenarios authored as design records only; no player study or encounter execution.

## Probe C — Does a preparation choice stay a choice?

**Frontier:** optional loot/path selection → durable bounded consequence → branch-to-branch operations.

- **Hypotheses:** C1 both direct and preparation-supported routes are credible; C2 preparation is mandatory because its benefit dominates its cost; C3 it is useless or its target is absent.
- **Setup/control:** one fixed ending, one optional operation, two contrasting build/resource states; unchanged ending as baseline. On paper first, then a disposable native fixture if authorized later.
- **Action/observations:** compare whole-route resource expenditure, actions, exposure, chosen approach and abandoned attempts. Test target absent, already generated, hero changed, save/reload, operation repeated and source lost.
- **Acceptance:** context explains why rational players differ; a completed operation changes the specified later encounter once; absence/abandonment has an explicit fair outcome. It must not force a longer route onto every normal run.
- **Failure interpretation:** an automatic best choice rejects the particular cost/benefit configuration. It does not require a new currency or prove all expedition consequences are bad.
- **Status:** proposed.

## Probe D — Can recognition be proven and credited once?

**Frontier:** historical IDs/events → replay-safe, correctly scoped completion → Atlas, Charter Hall and shared challenges.

- **Hypotheses:** D1 available evidence proves the selected predicate and human recipients; D2 snapshots prove a weaker fact; D3 host ownership incorrectly stands in for each controller.
- **Setup/control:** synthetic event/record fixtures in temporary storage. A simple terminal solo Orb escape is the control; a claimed rescue without a rescue event is a deliberately unsupported comparison. No grants in the live account archive.
- **Actions:** ingest a valid result twice, out of order and after restart; include partial snapshots, late join, leave, downed hero, spectator, host and multiple distinct authenticated controllers. Include wizard/QA and incompatible manifest versions as ineligible cases.
- **Acceptance:** exact supported predicates complete once for exactly the eligible recipients. A rescue, carrier handoff or encounter interrupt stays unknown when its event evidence does not exist. Failure does not mark a valid result processed prematurely; replay can recover safely.
- **Failure interpretation:** a missing action event blocks that achievement, not basic recorded rune/escape recognition. Existing rating dedup is a control to inspect, not a substitute reward ledger.
- **Status:** proposed; current records/statistics sources were inspected.

## Probe E — What does split-floor cooperation really permit?

**Frontier:** independent floor clocks → explicit linked objective time → Planar Expeditions without parking exploits or deadlocks.

- **Hypotheses:** E1 persistent completion/commitment works across frozen floors; E2 an unattended objective can be paused indefinitely to trivialize risk; E3 changing the clock breaks native timers or recovery.
- **Setup/control:** two disposable humans on two normal floors; known native cloud/entity timer and ordinary safe actions as controls. First reproduce the existing independent-floor contract before adding a proposed objective.
- **Actions:** leave one floor idle while acting on the other, activate/complete a proposed objective, disconnect its owner, travel, unload/reload both floors, save/restart and change recipients. Repeat on a special Pan realm before claiming compatibility there.
- **Acceptance:** clocks/effects obey their declared scope; completion persists without requiring simultaneous wall time; source loss/abandonment cannot strand another floor. Any expedition clock is explicitly new and does not silently advance unrelated entities.
- **Failure interpretation:** failed ordinary control is an environment/regression problem; timer freezing rejects the proposed pressure model, not independent exploration. A persistent action/commit model remains a useful narrower fallback.
- **Status:** proposed; existing source and prior validation documents inspected, no fresh clock gameplay performed.

## Probe F — Does party scale produce meaningful allocation?

**Frontier:** full XP sharing, shared gold, effects and trade → intentional party encounter budgets → co-op content without an arms race.

- **Hypotheses:** F1 2/4 heroes enable new plans; F2 extra actions/effects overwhelm threats; F3 distance or downing creates unintended XP/credit incentives.
- **Setup/control:** same native encounter family and declared rules for one, two and four heroes, with equivalent starting-combo policy. A solo encounter is a baseline, not a claim of equal difficulty from the same seed.
- **Actions/observations:** inspect awarded XP, off-floor XP, gold, transferred items, support actions, consumables, meaningful threat turns, idle time and downs. Compare diverse compositions; test a disconnected and a downed member as distinct boundary cases.
- **Acceptance:** players make different target/space/resource decisions; no mandatory role; group capability and rewards are explained and recorded. Determine any balance adjustment from measured mechanisms rather than an arbitrary HP multiplier.
- **Failure interpretation:** insufficient telemetry is inconclusive; a dominant burst composition guides a specific encounter/effect adjustment, not removal of all support or XP sharing.
- **Status:** proposed. Source confirms full awarded XP sharing to non-downed peers without a floor filter in the inspected awarding path.

## Probe G — What ends a cooperative run?

**Frontier:** existing Orb and terminal results → explicit expedition outcome → safe extraction content and reliable recognition.

- **Hypotheses:** G1 current native behavior supports the desired party finish; G2 first escape closes peers' opportunity; G3 per-hero terminal records and server party completion disagree.
- **Setup/control:** isolated near-escape native fixture with one hero as positive control, then two humans with different positions. Establish current behavior before proposing a physical carrier or changed victory rule.
- **Actions:** pick up Orb; inspect shared/individual state; move heroes onto separate floors; escape with one; leave another alive/downed/disconnected; save/resume at supported boundaries; inspect native terminal records and rating aggregation.
- **Acceptance:** actual terminal behavior is documented precisely; no eligible human loses progress through a misleading action; all public result and proposed award predicates agree. Proposed relay rules additionally need tested handoff, source loss and departure behavior.
- **Failure interpretation:** mismatched outcomes block an extraction reward design until corrected or scoped more narrowly. They do not make native solo escape invalid.
- **Status:** proposed. Current stats defines a party win as at least one hero escaping with the Orb and waits for all roster terminal records; this is a source-backed analytical policy, not a fresh multiplayer terminal observation.

## Probe H — Which metagame makes the next run more interesting?

**Frontier:** recorded history → player-selected future purpose → useful persistence without compulsory grinding.

- **Hypotheses:** H1 mastery/company goals improve the next choice; H2 players mainly want world response; H3 only permanent power feels worthwhile; H4 the extra setup is a chore.
- **Setup/control:** three paper continuations from the same short/failed run: ordinary start screen, Atlas/company challenge, bounded campaign state. Include newcomer, ordinary-win, extended and mixed-experience group contexts.
- **Action/observations:** ask what the player would attempt next, why, how many interactions it takes and what they expect to lose or retain. Show an explicit permanent-power alternative as a different ruleset rather than biasing the comparison with hidden tradeoffs.
- **Acceptance:** the selected model changes the next chosen action and remains understandable with direct launch. A newcomer can contribute immediately; an early failure does not require repeat death farming. Record which audience chose which model rather than claiming a universal winner.
- **Failure interpretation:** a cosmetic-only reward feeling weak does not prove endless power growth is necessary; better world/goal consequences remain a competing explanation.
- **Status:** proposed; no preference study conducted.

## Probe I — Can campaign history change safely and recoverably?

**Frontier:** verified expedition result → atomic bounded campaign transition → Company Campaign.

- **Hypotheses:** I1 exactly one chapter transition can configure a new run; I2 retries/overlap duplicate state/rewards; I3 setbacks make recovery impossible or membership changes steal credit.
- **Setup/control:** finite three-chapter paper/state model; a terminal completed solo chapter as control, missing evidence and an unowned expedition as ineligible cases. No account power or real inventory transfer.
- **Actions:** concurrent completions, duplicate result, crash after writing result but before acknowledgement, version migration, source save deletion, member departure, newcomer entry and campaign archive/reset.
- **Acceptance:** one authoritative versioned transition; progress is recoverable after uncertain delivery; unlocked chapter configurations are explicit; departure/visibility rules are coherent; setbacks have a valid recovery/terminal path. New native runs start under a fixed manifest.
- **Failure interpretation:** an interrupted write is setup/delivery failure until recovery is checked. A duplicating transition rejects that persistence mechanism; it does not prove a finite campaign is undesirable.
- **Status:** proposed. Existing public records do not establish this mutation contract.

## What to defer

Defer new full branches until their decision role and existing Forest/Blade content are characterized. The selected campaign explicitly explores transferable equipment, personal materials, contributed guild buildings and improved starting preparation. Begin with one material/building and separate-return slice; defer full persistent-dungeon simulation, global simultaneous raid timers and elaborate material hierarchies beyond the proposed small palette until demonstrated requirements support them.
